# The satellite data market: who's who

Who actually makes money in the satellite data market, and which companies will a consultant meet? The market has three roles. Some companies collect and sell the data. Others turn that data into answers. And then there are the buyers - and overseas, the largest buyers are national governments and defence agencies. In this report we name the main players in each role, show how the market is organised, how to read the headline numbers, and what a consultant should take from it.

## What this report covers

Here is what this report covers. First, the three roles in the market - the companies that sell the data, the companies that turn it into answers, and the buyers. Second, the main overseas players in each role, grouped by what they actually do. And third, how to read the headline revenue and backlog numbers you will see quoted, and what a consultant should take away from all of it.

## Who collects and sells the data

Start with the companies that collect and sell the raw data. The clearest way to sort them is by their sensor. First, high-resolution optical - the sharpest pictures from large satellites - led by Vantor, which was called Maxar until October 2025, and by Airbus in Europe. Second, small optical constellations that trade a little sharpness for frequent revisits, such as Planet, BlackSky and Satellogic. Third, radar, or synthetic aperture radar, which sees at night and straight through cloud - here Finland's ICEYE leads, alongside Capella and Umbra in the United States. And fourth, newer sensors that measure what the eye cannot see - Pixxel for hyperspectral, GHGSat for methane, and HawkEye 360 for radio signals.

## Who turns the data into answers

The second role is turning that data into answers. This matters because an end user - an insurer, a utility, a city - wants to know how many buildings flooded, not to own a radar image. So a whole layer of companies sits downstream. There are cloud and mapping platforms, such as Google Earth Engine, Esri and Microsoft's Planetary Computer, where the data is stored and analysed. There are marketplaces, such as UP42 and SkyFi, that let you search and order imagery from many operators in one place. And there are specialist analytics firms that package the data for a single industry. The company that sells the pixels and the company that delivers the answer are usually not the same.

## Who buys

The third role is the buyers, and this is where overseas markets differ most from the picture people expect. The growth is driven by defence and national governments, not by private companies. One concrete example is August 2026, when the United States National Reconnaissance Office awarded radar contracts to Capella, ICEYE's US arm and Umbra - and that followed an evaluation phase that began back in 2022, so this is operational procurement, not a pilot. Alongside this runs what the industry calls sovereign demand, where governments want their own satellites or guaranteed capacity rather than depending on another country. Commercial buyers in insurance, agriculture and infrastructure are real, but today they are smaller and growing more slowly.

## How to read the headline numbers

Now, how to read the numbers without being misled. Planet is the listed Earth-observation company with the largest revenue - about three hundred and eight million dollars in the year to January 2026, with a backlog near nine hundred million. But the word largest hides how small this industry still is. ICEYE reports over two hundred and fifty million euros of revenue for 2025 and a backlog of one and a half billion euros - though those figures are unaudited, and because ICEYE is private, they are the company's own numbers, not an audited filing. When a company says it runs the world's largest radar constellation, that is its own claim, and satellite counts often mix the number launched with the number still working in orbit. And names change fast - Maxar became Vantor in October 2025, so always check the date on any source.

## What a consultant should take from this

So what should a consultant take from this? First, no single company does everything well, so the job is to match the right player to the client's actual question, not to pick a favourite vendor. Second, remember that the company selling the pixels and the company giving the answer are usually different firms - decide which one the client really needs. Third, the headline growth is driven by defence and government demand, so do not quote it to a client as the size of the commercial market. And fourth, because names, owners and satellite counts change within months, every figure in a proposal needs a date and a source next to it.
